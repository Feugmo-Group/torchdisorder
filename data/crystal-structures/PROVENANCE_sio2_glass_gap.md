# `sio2_glass_gap.cif` — provenance

A **published, physically validated** amorphous SiO₂ model, downloaded rather than
generated here. It replaces `sio2_glass.cif`, which is not a glass (see below).

## Source

L. C. Erhard, J. Rohrer, K. Albe, V. L. Deringer,
*"A machine-learned interatomic potential for silica and its relation to empirical models"*,
**npj Computational Materials 8, 90 (2022)**.
DOI: [10.1038/s41524-022-00768-w](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41524-022-00768-w)

Data archive: [Zenodo 10.5281/zenodo.6353684](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.6353684)
File within archive: `sio2_potential_data/amorphous/large/gap.data`
Archive MD5 (verified on download): `6a16de69b5e17fd18160d9b55972a3e1`

**License: CC BY 4.0** — reuse permitted with attribution. Cite the paper above in any
publication that uses this structure.

Generated by the authors via GAP melt-quench at 10¹³ K/s. Converted here from LAMMPS
`data` format to CIF with ASE (`Z_of_type={1: 8, 2: 14}` — type 1 is O at mass 16,
type 2 is Si at mass 28, per the file's own `Masses` block).

## Why the old files could not be used

| file | ρ | ⟨CN⟩ | O–Si–O | problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `sio2_glass.cif` (this repo) | 2.20 | 3.02 | ±21.2° | 298 Si–O contacts below 1.4 Å, min 0.185 Å — atoms overlap, no first shell |
| `sio2_glass.cif` (on yemba) | 2.634 | 4.000 | ±0.91° | byte-for-byte the **crystal**, merely misnamed |

Note the diagnostic: the broken file has a *wider* angular spread than a real glass
(±21.2° vs ±5.38°). Disorder alone is not evidence of a glass — it is equally the
signature of a structure that has fallen apart. Density is no help either; the broken
file has exactly the right density. Only the coordination plateau separates the two.
